Equatorial Pacific · two equations

Recharge oscillator

El Niño, in the model Fei-Fei Jin wrote down in 1997. A deep western thermocline is stored heat. It warms the eastern Pacific. That warmth drives westerly winds, and those winds drain the heat off the equator. The drained ocean is what turns El Niño into La Niña. La Niña’s stronger trades pile the heat back up, and the cycle starts again.

Jin’s equations

dTE/dt = R TE + γ hW − εn (hW + b TE)3 + γ ξ1 + ξ2

dhW/dt = −r hW − α b TE − α ξ1

R = γ b − c,   b = b0 μ,   hE = hW + b TE

σ = (R − r) / 2,   ω = √[α b γ − (R + r)2 / 4]

What each term is doing now

This is Jin’s conceptual model only (Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences, 1997, Part I, eqs. 2.6, 3.1, and 3.4). Wind, upwelling, and the Bjerknes feedback are inside the coefficients. The small Ekman term δsb′ is left out, as Jin does after eq. 2.6. There is no seasonal cycle and no extra warm-cold asymmetry beyond the cubic thermocline term. States are integrated in his nondimensional units and shown in °C, meters, and years.

Eastern temperature and western heat

Coral is the eastern Pacific temperature anomaly TE. Gold is the western thermocline anomaly hW, the recharge. Heat peaks before the warm event.
